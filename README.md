# A Guide to Validating Approximate Unlearning for Regulatory Compliance

### Abstract

Approximate machine unlearning techniques have been developed to remove the influence of undesirable data from a model, thereby achieving regulatory goals concerning user privacy and public safety while avoiding the cost of retraining. However, thorough work on validating unlearning, i.e. providing evidence that unlearning has succeeded, is less explored. The literature has introduced a litany of validation metrics to measure the quality of forgetting, though there has been little comparable work on the information value of these metrics, or practical guidance on how regulators can use them to vet if a model meets compliance goals.

This research seeks to provide a comprehensive guide as to which metrics to use to validate unlearning and demonstrate a model's compliance with regulations. To do this, we (1) gather a large list of metrics common in the unlearning literature and discuss their connections to important regulatory goals, (2) discuss the practical advantages and disadvantages of metrics for the particular use case of regulatory compliance, (3) empirically test relationships between metrics to expose redundancies, and (4) offer shortlists of metrics to prioritize in each unlearning setting. We are argue that many metrics are undesirable for compliance attestation due to practical limitations. Our results also highlight that many metrics are superfluous, measuring essentially the same information about the unlearned model’s state as others, and the preferred shortlists of metrics differ across unlearning settings. This work builds on other contributions bridging the gap between the unlearning literature and the needs of regulators, pushing machine unlearning closer to being a viable tool for AI trust and safety.
