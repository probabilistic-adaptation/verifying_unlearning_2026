# A Guide to Validating Approximate Unlearning for Regulatory Compliance

### Abstract

Machine unlearning has developed rapidly in recent years, alongside a growing desire for machine learning techniques that facilitate the preservation of user privacy, public safety, and other regulatory goals, while mitigating the cost of retraining from scratch. Despite a rich literature on the development of approximate unlearning methods to meet these needs, the validation of unlearning, i.e. providing formal or informal evidence that unlearning has succeeded, is less explored. Up to now, the field has leveraged a wide variety of validation metrics, though there has been little comparable work on the information value of some metrics over others. What’s more, there is little guidance for regulators on how to practically leverage such metrics to vet whether a given model meets their compliance goals.

This research focuses on answering the question, "_which metrics across the unlearning literature would be most useful for demonstrating a model’s compliance?_" To this end, we gather a comprehensive set of metrics from the unlearning literature, discuss their connections to important regulatory goals, and empirically test the relationships between them across a variety of unlearning settings, model architectures, and benchmarks. We discuss the practical advantages and disadvantages of some metrics over others and how the right metrics to use change in different unlearning settings. We find that (1) many metrics used across the literature are superfluous, measuring essentially the same information about the unlearned model’s state as others, and (2) we point to the most-valuable metrics in each setting, which regulators can prioritize for future audit work. This work seeks to bridge the gap between the unlearning literature and the needs of regulators, furthering the canon of work making machine unlearning a viable tool for AI trust and safety.
